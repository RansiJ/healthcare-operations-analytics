01 / THE BUSINESS QUESTION

Where should an operations team look more closely?

Which acute care hospitals with emergency services show unfavorable ED operations or inpatient communication signals relative to other eligible hospitals of the same type in the same state? I built the comparisons to help an operations team decide where to ask for more recent local evidence. The data cannot explain why a hospital differs from its peers.

Hospital General Information

Hospital identity, state, type, ownership and emergency-service availability establish the population. Snapshot attributes are not reconstructed historical attributes.

Timely and Effective Care

OP_18b: ED visit median minutes, excluding transfer and psychiatric/mental-health cases. OP_22: published percent leaving before being seen. Lower is favorable for both.

HCAHPS Patient Survey

H_COMP_1_A_P: percent reporting nurses always communicated well during an inpatient stay. Higher is favorable. It is not an ED satisfaction measure.

05 / FINDINGS & DECISIONS

What I found and what I would check next

  1. Across 2,632 eligible hospitals, the median of published ED visit durations is 170 minutes. The middle half of hospital scores falls between 140 and 204 minutes. Each hospital counts equally; this is not a pooled patient waiting time.
  2. The median published left-before-seen percentage is 2% (IQR 1-3%) among 2,588 eligible hospitals. 338 report 0% (13.1%); rounded published zeros should not be interpreted as verified absence of individual events.
  3. The nurse-communication median is 78% (IQR 75-81%) across 2,496 eligible hospitals. Reporting notes and survey-sample rules exclude 181 other numeric scores. This reduction in coverage matters when interpreting inpatient experience alongside the separate ED measures.
  4. Peer comparisons are available for 2,567, 2,522 and 2,430 hospitals on OP_18b, OP_22 and nurse communication respectively. Another 197 eligible hospital-measure records lack ten other same-state peers. I left those comparisons unavailable rather than substituting a national benchmark.
  5. The quartile rule flags 659/2,567 OP_18b comparisons (25.7%), 493/2,522 OP_22 comparisons (19.5%) and 541/2,430 nurse-communication comparisons (22.3%). These shares describe a relative selection rule, not the prevalence of poor clinical care. Rounded scores and ties also affect how many hospitals cross a threshold.

Review ED flow locally

Use eligible OP_18b/OP_22 flags to request more recent arrival, staffing and throughput data. Verify case mix and local processes before considering capacity or workflow changes.

Review communication and evidence gaps

For inpatient communication flags, examine local survey comments and communication routines. For excluded or sparse-peer records, improve the evidence base before making a relative-performance claim.

06 / METHODOLOGY & LIMITATIONS

How I kept the comparisons consistent

I used PySpark to join the three fixed CMS sources, validate their types and dates, and retain missing values and reporting notes. I kept hospital attributes and measurements in separate tables so each measure retained its own period. Spark SQL expresses the peer-selection rules and exact percentile calculation in queries that can be inspected separately. A flag requires an eligible target, ten other peers, and a strict crossing of Q3 for ED measures or Q1 for HCAHPS. Published percentages are retained on the 0-100 scale; gaps use percentage points.

Prior Phase 2 evidence records 27 passing tests, 22,557 independently checked percentiles and two equivalent model runs. For this report, the selected run's SUCCESS.json, quality-report hash, all five export hashes and row counts were verified before analysis. This report build read existing exports; it did not rerun Spark, the pipeline or the original test suite.

  • OP_22 covers calendar 2024; OP_18b and HCAHPS cover October 2024-September 2025. These are historical, cross-sectional measures, not current operational monitoring.
  • ED operations and inpatient experience represent different patient populations. Their co-occurrence does not establish causation.
  • State/type matching does not adjust for hospital size, case mix, teaching status, staffing or rurality. Missingness and survey nonresponse can be selective.
  • Quartile rules generate relative review flags by design. Ties, rounding and eligibility affect flag shares. Peer IQRs are not confidence intervals.
  • Medians across hospitals are not pooled patient medians. Rounded percentages and period survey totals do not supply item-level patient denominators.
  • Databricks execution and a Power BI dashboard are postponed. This is a Python/matplotlib static HTML report; no cloud execution, ML or inferential test is claimed.
